CG Semi began commercial production at its Sanand, Gujarat G1 OSAT on 4 July 2026 — packaging and testing chips with peak capacity up to ~300 million units a year inside a ₹7,600 crore G1+G2 plan. This matters more for India’s AI economy than another model demo: without packaging capacity, “Make AI in India” is a slide, not a supply chain.
</Answer>

Model launches trend on LinkedIn. Packaging plants show up in BSE filings. If you buy edge AI devices, automotive controllers, or industrial boards in India, Sanand’s OSAT cluster is the story that changes lead times and BOM risk — not the keynote.

## What Changed

- **4 July 2026:** CG Semi announced commercial production at G1 OSAT, Sanand, in the presence of the PM ([company/BSE release](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachLive/313b74ac-e0b8-48b1-8925-5c31f65c6b20.pdf)).
- JV: **CG Power (Murugappa)** majority (~92.3%), with **Renesas** and **Stars Microelectronics**.
- G1 peak capacity: up to **~300 million units/year** (company); press also cited ~**200 million** initial annual run-rate scaling higher.
- Capex plan: **₹7,600+ crore** over five years for **G1 + G2**; G2 under development.
- Third packaging plant online in Sanand cluster in 2026 after Micron and Kaynes Semicon (press consensus).

## The Details

### OSAT in plain English

Think of a chip like a cooked meal:

1. **Fab** = kitchen that cooks the silicon wafer (TSMC-class work — India is still early here).
2. **OSAT** = plating, boxing, and taste-testing before the meal ships — **assembly, packaging, test**.
3. **Board/OEM** = the restaurant that serves the device.

India can import wafers and still capture packaging jobs, test IP, and logistics control. That is what Sanand is building.

But here’s what others won’t tell you: **OSAT capacity is not H100 supply.** Packaging automotive MCUs and IoT SoCs does not magically cut your Bengaluru training cluster’s Nvidia bill next quarter. It does harden India’s electronics stack so edge AI devices, EVs, and industrial controllers are less hostage to overseas back-end queues.

### Timeline to impact for AI builders

- **2026–27:** Qualification wins for automotive/industrial/IoT packages; dual-source options for India OEMs.
- **2027–28:** G2 scale — meaningful if you’re an electronics manufacturer or EMS buyer.
- **Frontier AI accelerators:** Still gated by advanced packaging (CoWoS-class) and HBM geopolitics. Watch ISM advanced-packaging roadmaps separately from G1’s legacy + advanced package mix (QFN/QFP, FC-BGA/FC-CSP cited in press).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an OSAT facility?

OSAT means Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test — the back-end step that packages and tests chips after silicon is fabricated elsewhere. It is not a wafer fab.

### What capacity does CG Semi’s Gujarat plant have?

CG Semi’s G1 Sanand plant lists peak capacity up to about 300 million units per year in its July 2026 commercial-production release; some press cited ~200 million initial annual output scaling toward higher targets as lines ramp.

### How much is CG Semi investing?

The JV plans over ₹7,600 crore (~$870 million) across five years for G1 and G2 OSAT facilities, with India Semiconductor Mission fiscal support on eligible capex.

### When will this cut AI hardware costs in India?

Expect gradual impact over 2026–2028 on automotive, industrial, and IoT packages first. Frontier GPU economics still run through global foundry/HBM supply — OSAT helps electronics BOM and supply resilience more than training-cluster sticker prices overnight.
